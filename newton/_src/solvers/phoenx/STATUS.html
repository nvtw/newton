What currently limits performance

  • Physics remains the largest work category; reduced advance/publish and contact row build/solve are major components.
  • Learner dense forward, weight gradients, and recurrent forward/backward are the other large blocks.
  • Host launch gaps are negligible: Nsight measured about 0.1–0.2 ms around a roughly 420 ms iteration.
  • Environment bookkeeping and state import/export are each below 1%.

Current investigation

  • G1 has 17 shape-pair columns but only four compatible physical body pairs per world.
  • A contact-stable pair measured 1.867M grouped versus 1.623M control physics steps/s (+15%).
  • Blocker: current grouping merges mixed-material compound shapes using friction from the first shape. Fix and regress that root cause before enabling fleets.

Rejected or parked directions

  • BF16/FP16 paths: isolated kernels improved, but end-to-end throughput or learning gates did not.
  • Fewer PPO replays, less relaxation, or fresh-policy serialization: failed learning or stability gates.
  • Patch mass caches and host/stream scheduling: neutral or too small end to end.
  • Scene-specialized kernels: rejected; PhoenX remains general.

Correctness findings still open

  • Free-flight joint trajectories are close (0.0027 rad RMSE over ten control steps); ground contact causes immediate divergence.
  • Both systems satisfy support-force and Coulomb bounds. Soft-pyramidal versus patch-Coulomb models are a plausible difference, not a proven bug.
  • Projected maximal changes the error rather than consistently improving it.
Rule: reason from physical invariants and regression evidence. Matching nanoG1 is not itself proof of correctness.

Validation discipline

  • Primary metric: multi-seed wall-clock time to a frozen policy gate; throughput and traces are secondary.
  • Behavior-changing candidates require multi-seed evidence.
  • Focused G1, H1, reduced-Ant, ownership, and maximal patch tests pass; scoped pre-commit passes.
