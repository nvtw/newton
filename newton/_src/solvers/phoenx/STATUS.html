PhoenX physics and training are faster than at the start of this chase. The latest contact-grouping experiment was not retained: it accelerated isolated physics but slightly slowed full training.

Retained wins

  • Packed FP16x2 contact rows: about +1.1% G1 training.
  • Compact reduced-factor storage and DOF-type factor split: less memory and up to +2.8% isolated physics.
  • Packed contact gather and selective launch tuning: substantial isolated physics wins, about +1% training where measured.
  • Dense learner tiling and tiled Muon: about +6% and +3.3% G1 training respectively.
  • Reusable contact graphs and topology caching: generic gains across homogeneous robot fleets.
  • Ant PPO work restored reliable learning and produced a large shared-PPO gain; current smoke remains finite near 0.93M samples/s.

Latest rejected result

Body-pair grouping reduced G1 columns 73,728 → 16,384 and improved isolated physics 16–23%, but production training measured 1.234M versus 1.244M control. Nsight showed the dominant reduced row build and solve were unchanged, while sorting added work. The optimization was removed.

What limits us now

  • Reduced advance/publish, contact-row construction, and contact solve dominate PhoenX physics.
  • The learner dense/recurrent passes are the other major training cost; host launch gaps and bookkeeping are small.
  • Nsight Compute shows the contact kernels are dependency/eligibility limited, not tensor-core or raw-DRAM limited. Wider loads, extra streams, row caches, and patch-row compression have already failed end-to-end validation.
  • The next credible solver target is a general pipeline/layout change that improves row construction or removes a full materialization pass—not a scene special case or a small arithmetic tweak.

Correctness and learning

  • Commit e549a1ea2 fixes mixed-material grouping and per-contact friction scaling with end-to-end regressions.
  • Neither implementation always learns faster. Single seeds and shipped checkpoints are not reliable evidence; the proper metric is multi-seed wall-clock time to a frozen gate.
  • Physics candidates must preserve invariants and pass G1 plus cross-scene tests. Matching nanoG1 alone is not proof of correctness.
