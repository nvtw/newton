Three current 75.5M-sample runs take 58.4–59.1 s of training. Short paired overlap benchmarks can reach 1.590M samples/s, but the uninterrupted number is the honest time-to-quality comparison.

Current quality result

Seeds 11, 29, and 42 are stable with zero falls at 75.5M samples, but none passes the frozen gate. Battery performance is 0.745–0.765; all three under-track the +0.8 m/s forward command and exceed the torso angular-velocity limit. nanoG1’s shipped seed-42 checkpoint was re-run locally and passes all six frozen checks at 75M.

Retained wins

  • Isolated learner: about 114 to 99.5 ms/update through parallel priority sampling and reductions.
  • Optional BF16/cuBLAS path: about +10% G1 and -23% Ant wall clock; tested Warp fallback remains available.
  • Reduced/contact data-layout work gives smaller general physics wins without changing reference quality.
  • Warmed Ant learner time improved from 2.272 to 2.084 s and still learns finite forward locomotion.

What remains

  • A moderate action-rate penalty relaxation made seed 42 worse, so it is rejected.
  • The train-to-gate default reset worlds and recurrent state at each measurement boundary. The fix makes uninterrupted training the default and adds a regression.
  • Next: explain the repeated fast-forward acquisition gap from solver/task dynamics and learner semantics, then test only first-principles changes across multiple seeds.
  • For physics, Nsight points to reduced advance/publish, contact-row construction, and patch solve—not collision micro-tuning.

Correctness guardrails

  • Production physics matches the 20×8 PhoenX reference to 0.00059 rad joint-position RMS and 0.35 mm base-state RMS with zero falls.
  • Mirror loss and BF16 GEMM forward/backward paths have independent parity coverage.
  • Learning claims require uninterrupted multi-seed runs and the frozen gate; a short throughput sample or one lucky seed is not proof.
