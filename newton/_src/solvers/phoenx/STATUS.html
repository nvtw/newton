Reusing BF16 forward inputs and weights in the hand-written MinGRU backward reached 1.402M samples/s versus a 1.396M reverse control and the local 1.393M nanoG1 reference. The margin is small; practical parity is the robust claim.

Corrected diagnosis

Corrected isolation measures about 279 ms per PhoenX rollout versus roughly 270 ms for nanoG1, and about 200 ms per PhoenX update versus roughly 94 ms for nanoG1. CUDA-graph overlap hides most of that learner difference. Short eager training with diagnostics has finite rewards, losses, KL, and actions.

Retained wins

  • Compact reduced-factor storage and DOF-type factor split: less memory and up to +2.8% isolated physics.
  • Packed contact gather and selective launch tuning: substantial isolated physics wins, about +1% training where measured.
  • Dense learner tiling, tiled Muon, and BF16 MinGRU forward: about +6%, +3.3%, and +3.6% G1 training respectively.
  • PufferLib-style reuse of pre-update V-trace values: +6.2% G1 training and consistent PPO snapshots.
  • Update-first launch plus high-priority rollout blocks: +1.5% G1 wall clock.
  • Reuse BF16 forward buffers in MinGRU backward: -1.8% isolated update and +0.7% overlap.
  • Reusable contact graphs and topology caching: generic gains across homogeneous robot fleets.
  • Ant PPO work restored reliable learning; the current smoke remains finite near 0.93M samples/s.

What limits us now

  • The measured +0.6% lead is within observed run variance; practical wall-clock parity is established.
  • nanoG1’s 75M claim is one deterministic seed (42), not a seed distribution. In a fresh five-seed PhoenX 39.3M screen, seed 42 was best: 0.732 versus 0.616–0.683.
  • Dense forward/weight-gradient passes, MinGRU, and Muon Newton-Schulz matrix work dominate the learner.
  • PufferLib still wins isolated learner time through device-wide CUTLASS/cuBLAS contractions and fewer intermediate kernels.
  • Next: general contraction fusion/device-wide GEMM experiments and multi-seed time-to-quality.

Correctness guardrails

  • FP16 contact rows were removed earlier: their roughly 1% gain did not justify the complexity. Current production rows are FP32.
  • Commit e549a1ea2 fixes mixed-material grouping and per-contact friction scaling with end-to-end regressions.
  • Learning comparisons require multiple seeds and a frozen quality gate; one fast seed or checkpoint is not proof.
