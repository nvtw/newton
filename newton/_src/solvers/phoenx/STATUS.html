Current quality result

The retained seed-42 policy passes at 141.56M samples after 84.18 seconds of training, with battery score 0.901 and zero falls. Seed 43 previously failed to acquire a good forward policy by 157.29M samples, so the seed-42 wall time is not yet a robust expectation. nanoG1's published 58.9-second result is also seed 42 only.

The historical 91.75M PhoenX result is not a current baseline: it predates fixed-order deterministic updates and two recurrent-PPO replay corrections. It may still be a useful artifact, but it cannot support a modern sample-efficiency claim.

Root cause is now narrower: with nanoG1's dense-reward coefficients, seed 43 becomes a zero-fall walker by 89–94M samples. A gradual regularization stage retains tracking above 0.90 while improving jerk 0.293 → 0.199, torso motion 0.338 → 0.240, and yaw error 0.357 → 0.168. No checkpoint passes every strict threshold simultaneously, so this remains a diagnostic rather than a retained recipe.

What remains

  • Robust sample efficiency is the main gap: seed 42 needs 66.6M more samples than nanoG1, and seed 43 fails the gate by 157.3M.
  • Strong early regularization blocks acquisition; applying it late exposes a torso-versus-action-smoothness tradeoff. Larger action penalties eventually suppress forward tracking rather than solve the tradeoff.
  • A source-level audit finds the core PhoenXRL Puffer-style V-trace, priority replay, normalization, ratio, and value-update semantics aligned with the pinned PufferLib source.
  • The 29-output/12-control layout matches pinned nanoG1. Its 17 inactive stochastic outputs distort early PPO KL, but a 12-output prototype only gained 1.4% throughput and made policy acquisition less robust. It was rejected and removed.
  • The existing adaptive-KL controller also overreacted at minibatch granularity and blocked acquisition. It is not a suitable automatic default.
  • The fresh Nsight capture at 1.686M samples/s confirms reduced advance/publish (13.2%), packed contact rows (9.1%), patch solves (9.4% combined), MinGRU (10.0%), and reduced factor passes (8.1%) as the broad costs.
  • PhoenX PBT currently reuses live workers but schedules them serially. A fixed-world-budget batched population with early elimination is feasible, but must prove better end-to-quality before production use.
  • Fixed G1 eager PBT discarding interval statistics after its first cycle, which made subsequent fitness -inf. G1 graph continuation and both Anymal paths were unaffected; split and uninterrupted same-seed checkpoints now match exactly.
  • Next: prototype fixed-budget policy racing and scale-invariant, confidence-gated learning control in a small environment before production G1 integration.
