Enabling the configured BF16 tensor-core forward in PufferMinGRU raised production G1 training from 1.240M to 1.284M samples/s. Physics rollout is close to nanoG1; the remaining wall-clock gap is mainly in the PPO update.

Corrected diagnosis

An experimental overlap benchmark had inherited non-production environment defaults. It now uses the production G1 recipe and update-first launch order, with a regression test. Corrected isolation now measures about 273 ms per PhoenX rollout versus roughly 270 ms for nanoG1, and 231 ms per PhoenX update versus roughly 94 ms for nanoG1. The BF16 MinGRU forward reduced the PhoenX update by about 7.7%.

Retained wins

  • Compact reduced-factor storage and DOF-type factor split: less memory and up to +2.8% isolated physics.
  • Packed contact gather and selective launch tuning: substantial isolated physics wins, about +1% training where measured.
  • Dense learner tiling, tiled Muon, and BF16 MinGRU forward: about +6%, +3.3%, and +3.6% G1 training respectively.
  • Reusable contact graphs and topology caching: generic gains across homogeneous robot fleets.
  • Ant PPO work restored reliable learning; the current smoke remains finite near 0.93M samples/s.

What limits us now

  • CUDA-graph overlap hides about 43% of the PhoenX update behind physics, but the exposed learner tail sets the remaining gap.
  • Dense forward/weight-gradient passes, MinGRU, and Muon Newton-Schulz matrix work dominate the learner.
  • PhoenX now uses BF16 tensor-core dense forward and weight gradients, but still repeats some casts and runs more separate Muon matrix passes than nanoG1/PufferLib.
  • Next: prototype persistent BF16 shadows and BF16/batched Muon, then gate on G1, cross-scene PPO, numerical checks, and learning.

Correctness guardrails

  • FP16 contact rows were removed earlier: their roughly 1% gain did not justify the complexity. Current production rows are FP32.
  • Commit e549a1ea2 fixes mixed-material grouping and per-contact friction scaling with end-to-end regressions.
  • Learning comparisons require multiple seeds and a frozen quality gate; one fast seed or checkpoint is not proof.
