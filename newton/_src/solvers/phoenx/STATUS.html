What currently limits performance

  • Physics remains the largest work category; reduced advance/publish and contact row build/solve are major components.
  • Learner dense forward, weight gradients, and recurrent forward/backward are the other large blocks.
  • Host launch gaps are negligible: Nsight measured about 0.1–0.2 ms around a roughly 420 ms iteration.
  • Environment bookkeeping and state import/export are each below 1%.

Current investigation

  • Use bounded Nsight traces to select the next broad kernel/data-layout target.
  • Favor homogeneous-fleet topology sharing and less repeated immutable data.
  • Validate every candidate end to end on G1 and at least one structurally different scene.
  • Audit every discovered correctness issue across all PhoenX solver paths.

Rejected or parked directions

  • Fresh-policy serialization: tracking improved slightly, but throughput fell 11.9% and angular stability worsened.
  • Forced BF16 tensor-core recurrent forward: emitted real MMA instructions but regressed end-to-end throughput; removed.
  • FP16×2 contact rows: isolated kernels improved, but a four-seed learning race produced no qualified pass; parked.
  • Fewer PPO replays or removing velocity relaxation: faster but failed frozen learning gates.
  • High-priority rollout stream and host batching: neutral or unjustified by measured launch gaps.
  • Cached patch effective masses: a separate pass lost 1.1–1.8%; exact in-kernel caching was neutral. Reusing the builder scalar gained 6–7% physics but only 1.08% training and changed reduction rounding, so it was removed rather than funding another seed race.
  • Scene-specialized robot kernels: rejected as overfitting; PhoenX remains a general solver.

Correctness findings still open

  • Free-flight PhoenX/nanoG1 joint trajectories are close: 0.0027 rad RMSE over ten control steps.
  • Ground contact causes immediate divergence, but both systems satisfy plausible support-force and Coulomb bounds.
  • The remaining difference is not classified as a bug: nanoG1 uses MuJoCo pyramidal soft constraints; PhoenX uses a patch Coulomb solve.
  • Projected maximal improves position agreement but worsens velocity agreement, so it is diagnostic evidence, not a replacement recommendation.
Rule: reason from physical invariants and regression evidence. Matching nanoG1 is not itself proof of correctness.

Validation discipline

  • Primary metric: reproducible multi-seed wall-clock time to a frozen qualified-policy gate.
  • Secondary metrics: steady training throughput, physics throughput, kernel shares, and open-loop trajectory comparisons.
  • No speed candidate is retained when it changes learning behavior without adequate multi-seed evidence.
  • Focused G1, H1, reduced-Ant, ownership, and maximal patch momentum tests pass for the latest fix.
  • Scoped pre-commit passes. Full-repository pre-commit still reports unrelated pre-existing lint/typo failures.
