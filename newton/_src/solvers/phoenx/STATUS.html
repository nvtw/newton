Device-wide BF16 contractions reach 1.570–1.576M samples/s around a 1.430M same-session Warp control. cuBLAS is optional; the existing Warp path remains the fallback.

Corrected diagnosis

Rollout remains about 279 ms versus roughly 270 ms for nanoG1. Device-wide GEMM cuts the isolated PhoenX update from about 166 ms to 112.6 ms; nanoG1 is roughly 94 ms. CUDA-graph overlap makes PhoenX faster end to end.

Retained wins

  • Compact reduced-factor storage and DOF-type factor split: less memory and up to +2.8% isolated physics.
  • Packed contact gather and selective launch tuning: substantial isolated physics wins, about +1% training where measured.
  • Dense learner tiling, tiled Muon, and BF16 MinGRU forward: about +6%, +3.3%, and +3.6% G1 training respectively.
  • PufferLib-style reuse of pre-update V-trace values: +6.2% G1 training and consistent PPO snapshots.
  • Update-first launch plus high-priority rollout blocks: +1.5% G1 wall clock.
  • Reuse BF16 forward buffers in MinGRU backward: -1.8% isolated update and +0.7% overlap.
  • Emit BF16 sequence gradients directly: -2.9% isolated update and about +1.8% production G1.
  • Batch three compatible Muon matrix passes: -8.4% isolated update and about +0.5% production G1.
  • Optional device-wide BF16 GEMM: -32% isolated G1 update, about +10% production G1, and -23% Ant wall clock.
  • Reusable contact graphs and topology caching: generic gains across homogeneous robot fleets.
  • Ant's 120-iteration accelerated smoke remains finite and learns forward locomotion.

What limits us now

  • Sample efficiency remains the larger gap: accelerated seed 42 passes the full gate at 131.07M samples, versus nanoG1’s single-seed 75M report.
  • Seed 11 misses at 100M under both cuBLAS and Warp; cuBLAS is slightly better there. Multi-seed time-to-quality is still required.
  • cuBLAS is not guaranteed by a CUDA runtime installation. Systems without it keep the tested Warp tensor-tile kernels.
  • Next: a shape/device-derived Warp kernel factory, plus multi-seed time-to-quality.

Correctness guardrails

  • FP16 contact rows were removed earlier: their roughly 1% gain did not justify the complexity. Current production rows are FP32.
  • Commit e549a1ea2 fixes mixed-material grouping and per-contact friction scaling with end-to-end regressions.
  • Mirror loss was audited against nanoG1’s exact pinned Puffer commit. Both deliberately stop gradients through the mirrored target and train the original branch; this is not a PhoenX mismatch.
  • Forward, weight-gradient, and input-gradient GEMMs match independent BF16-rounded references under CUDA graph capture.
  • Accelerated seed 42 passes the full held-out G1 gate at 131.07M samples with zero falls.
  • Learning comparisons require multiple seeds and a frozen quality gate; one fast seed or checkpoint is not proof.
