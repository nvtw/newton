What does #1 do?

Each launch solves one color of independent brick contacts: it reads contact data and body velocities, computes equal-and-opposite normal and friction impulses, and writes the resulting velocities. Later colors depend on those writes. With one 60 Hz tick, five substeps, and eight biased rounds, this trace contains 240,000 iteration launches in 600 frames, or 400 per rendered frame.

Where the time can move

Measured wins
Grid removed the old broad-phase bottleneckOn four towers, it visits about 3.6 million local pairs per pass against 36.5 million projected SAP candidates. Exact pair-set checks and CUDA graph replay passed. Stable OptiX throughput rose to about 49 FPS with three substeps.
Streams overlap, but the gain is modestPhysics and OptiX use separate CUDA streams; 79 of 80 ray launches overlap physics in the trace. Matched runs gained about 0.9 ms or 1.2 FPS. The renderer's TLAS rebuild also removed a late ray-launch stall.
Broader options
Implicit tree wins on mixed sizesCaptured broad-phase replay with one tower and 1,000 boxes of 0.8–1.6 m: implicit BVTT 0.269 ms, SAP 0.590 ms, grid 0.729 ms. All three returned identical candidate-pair sets. Focused tree tests passed moving bounds, filters, touching faces, forced queue overflow, append behavior, and captured replay. Two independent 2×2 trajectories matched bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.
Grid wins on uniform KaplaWith four towers (45,360 bricks and 383,640 candidate pairs), grid replay took 0.215 ms, implicit BVTT 0.515 ms, and SAP 2.512 ms. Short stable-solver end-to-end OptiX replay reached 45.07 FPS with the tree versus about 49 FPS with the grid. The grid has no correctness volume cap: oversized and out-of-range shapes use its exact fallback.
Intermittent slowdown is the current gateThe longest run completed 5,000 OptiX frames at 62.16 FPS after warm-up; two more unprofiled runs completed 1,000 and 1,200 frames at roughly 62–63 FPS. A separate 1,000-frame run dropped from about 60 to about 9 FPS at frame 493 and stayed slow. During normal sampled frames, physics occupied about 15–18 ms on its stream and the OptiX ray launch about 1.3 ms on the render stream. The failing run had the benchmark process as the only CUDA compute process, with 100% GPU utilization and stable clocks. The operation causing the intermittent collapse is still unverified.
Quality gate
Two substeps are fast but fail lateEvery 20-frame window exceeded 60 FPS over 1,000 OptiX frames, yet only 50.7–72.0% of bricks per tower stayed within 1 cm over the final 100 physics frames. Friction-anchor drift and speed rise before contacts disappear.
Two velocity passes close the quality gapOne 60 Hz collision tick, five temporal substeps, and two velocity relaxation passes per substep passed the 1,000-frame physics check: 99.79–99.99% stationary per tower, median RMS speed 0.00349–0.00360 m/s. One velocity pass reached 65.07 OptiX FPS but missed the RMS limit; the second pass fixes its residual motion. Two independent physics runs matched position, orientation, and both velocity hashes bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.
Three substeps remain the stable referenceAt 1,000 frames, 97.6–98.2% of bricks per tower stayed within 1 cm and median RMS speed was 0.00269–0.00283 m/s. Both grid and tree produced the same reported settling metrics; independent tree trajectories matched bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.

Scene bounds and object sizes

The grid has an exact fallback for oversized or out-of-range shapes, so its cell width affects speed, not correctness. The tree uses exact node bounds and needs no cell size or fixed scene volume. Its Morton ordering affects speed only. A direct per-shape tree walk also returned exact pairs, but was slower than the pair-tree traversal and was removed.

Verification · four towers in one world

Quality and determinism passed; rendered speed is intermittent

The one-tick, five-substep, two-velocity-pass schedule passed all four towers' 1,000-frame settling limits and repeated trajectory hashes matched bitwise. A 5,000-frame OptiX run averaged 62.16 FPS after warm-up without a slowdown; a separate 1,000-frame run collapsed to about 9 FPS partway through. Seven biased sweeps missed the quality limit, so eight remain required. The 60 FPS target remains unverified until the intermittent collapse is understood and removed.
