What does #1 do?

Each launch solves one color of independent brick contacts: it reads contact data and body velocities, computes paired normal and friction impulses, and writes the resulting velocities. Later colors depend on those writes. Across two 120 Hz ticks, three substeps, eight iterations, and regular colors plus overflow, this trace contains 38,400 iteration launches in 80 frames, or 480 per rendered frame.

Research track

Where the time can move

Measured wins
Grid removed the old broad-phase bottleneckOn four towers, it visits about 3.6 million local pairs per pass against 36.5 million projected SAP candidates. Exact pair-set checks and CUDA graph replay passed. Stable OptiX throughput rose to about 49 FPS with three substeps.
Streams overlap, but the gain is modestPhysics and OptiX use separate CUDA streams; 79 of 80 ray launches overlap physics in the trace. Matched runs gained about 0.9 ms or 1.2 FPS. The renderer's TLAS rebuild also removed a late ray-launch stall.
Current experiments
Implicit tree wins on mixed sizesCaptured broad-phase replay with one tower and 1,000 boxes of 0.8–1.6 m: implicit BVTT 0.269 ms, SAP 0.590 ms, grid 0.729 ms. All three returned identical candidate-pair sets. Focused tree tests passed moving bounds, filters, touching faces, forced queue overflow, append behavior, and captured replay. Two independent 2×2 trajectories matched bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.
Grid wins on uniform KaplaWith four towers (45,360 bricks and 383,640 candidate pairs), grid replay took 0.215 ms, implicit BVTT 0.515 ms, and SAP 2.512 ms. Short stable-solver end-to-end OptiX replay reached 45.07 FPS with the tree versus about 49 FPS with the grid. The grid has no correctness volume cap: oversized and out-of-range shapes use its exact fallback.
Improve active support contactsA conservative dormancy census found only 0.18–0.22% of contact points safe to skip. Local forward/reverse manifold sweeps failed long-run settling because neighboring shape pairs still need global propagation. The next design study targets cluster-resident solves across pairs rather than another iteration-count search.
Quality gate
Two substeps are fast but fail lateEvery 20-frame window exceeded 60 FPS over 1,000 OptiX frames, yet only 50.7–72.0% of bricks per tower stayed within 1 cm over the final 100 physics frames. Friction-anchor drift and speed rise before contacts disappear.
Three substeps remain the stable referenceAt 1,000 frames, 97.6–98.2% of bricks per tower stayed within 1 cm and median RMS speed was 0.00269–0.00283 m/s. Repeated physics trajectories matched bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.
Verification · four towers in one world

Speed reached; convergence remains

The two-substep, 1,000-frame OptiX run stayed above 60 FPS in all reported 20-frame windows (minimum 60.29 FPS), but its separate 1,000-frame physics run failed settling. A grid-plus-three-substep control stayed stable through 1,000 frames, isolating the problem to solver cadence. Grid and tree pair-set, graph replay, and short bitwise deterministic replay checks passed.
