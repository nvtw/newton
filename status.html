What does #1 do?

It processes one color of independent brick contacts, reads contact rows and velocities, computes collision and friction impulses, then writes updated body velocities directly. The next color depends on those writes. At 3 substeps and 8 iterations, this kernel launches 480 times per rendered frame: 38,400 launches across this trace.

Investigation log

What changed

Separate CUDA streams confirmedPhysics and OptiX already overlap. The old frame rate was dominated by physics and occasional ray-tracing stalls.
Renderer stall fixedAfter repeated TLAS refits, ray launch could jump from about 1 ms to 60 ms. A full rebuild after 16 refits kept rendering steady for 400 frames; the renderer regression test passes.
Large single world hybrid · 66 FPSIndependent colored contacts now update rigid bodies directly; only overflow contacts use split copies. This passes the settled tower test with 4 substeps and 8 iterations and reaches about 66 FPS across 400 OptiX frames. Seven iterations fail the jitter threshold.
Three substeps and local overflow copies · about 83 FPSThe hybrid path passes the jitter test with 3 substeps and 8 iterations. Each overflow contact initializes its own copy, then copies reduce directly into body velocities. A 1,000-frame OptiX run stayed above 80.72 FPS in every 20-frame window; at frame 800 it measured 84.12 FPS.
Long-run stability checkedAfter 1,000 physics frames, 97.1% of bricks moved less than 1 cm during the final 100 frames. Their median RMS speed was 0.00273 m/s, below the 0.007 m/s regression limit.
Conservation and determinism checkedA focused split-copy momentum test and the existing overflow force test pass. In a 40-frame tower comparison, fused and previous reductions produced bitwise identical positions and velocities; a repeated fused run matched bitwise too.
Color grouping screenedThe existing grouped-color path reached 2.87 FPS on Kapla with its required settings, versus roughly 60 FPS in the current physics-only path. Its 32-lane per-group schedule does not expose enough parallelism for this tower. The probe switches were removed.
Color cap tradeoff testedReducing the regular color cap from nine to six failed the settled tower test; the faster schedule moved too many contacts into the split overflow pass. The cap remains nine.
Remaining costThe repeated color solve costs 5.87 ms per frame. Copy broadcast no longer appears in the current top 10. The larger four-tower scene exposes a new broad-phase bottleneck.
Hardware counters capturedThe 2×2 run recorded 87,492 CUDA kernels. In its final two seconds, the graphics engine averaged 99.7% active, SMs 83.3% active, and SM issue throughput 12.0%. Profiling enabled: 36.38 FPS.

Busy GPU, limited issue throughput

CUDA kernels cover 97.9% of the 2×2 frame interval. Hardware counters now show 99.7% graphics-engine activity, 83.3% SM activity, and 12.0% SM issue throughput in the final two seconds. The contact kernel launches 1,504 blocks of 32 threads on 188 SMs, but each regular color has only 16–22k useful contacts against 48k launched lanes. The GPU stays occupied with work, yet the serialized color schedule and partially useful launches leave compute throughput available.
