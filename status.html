Kapla + Hoberman

One authored Kapla tower and ten jointed Hoberman spheres in one world. The sphere ring is wider so neighbors start clear of each other. Hoberman body, joint, and visual data live in a neutral Python module; the scene does not parse USD at runtime.

Unsprung structural reference

Stable mixed joint and contact scene

Before adding springs, the spaced one-tower, ten-sphere model passed a 600-frame GPU structural gate: zero of 11,340 bricks fell by more than 50 cm; maximum downward motion was 2.4 mm and maximum lateral motion was 32 mm. Maximum Hoberman attachment error was 4.744 mm and hinge-axis error 1.460°, with 2,835 sphere contacts at the final frame. The large single world uses overflow-only mass splitting and avoids a global copy broadcast/writeback intended for direct solves. Picking uses stiffness 300, damping 30, and a 20 g acceleration cap; interactive feel remains to be checked.

New · cross-hinge springs

The middle pivots pull toward their initial angles

Each sphere now springs all 120 revolute _mid joints, the crossing pivots between struts; inner and outer connector hinges remain free. The native position drives use 50 N·m/rad stiffness and 5 N·m·s/rad damping, with the authored initial angle as target. Across ten spheres this adds 1,200 spring drives. A 600-frame GPU run passed with zero fallen tower bricks; mean middle-hinge displacement from the initial angle fell from 57.9° unsprung to 52.1°. Maximum attachment error was 5.98 mm versus 4.74 mm unsprung, and maximum hinge-axis error was 1.93° versus 1.46°; visual review remains open. Idle-start matched 180-frame headless GL runs measured 51.2 rendered FPS with springs versus 53.5 without them; those recorded runs started below 5% GPU utilization. Future measurements use the below-10% start gate. The earlier 58.2 FPS peak was unsprung under a different run.

New · ordered grid kernel

A warp shares each dense cell’s pair loop

The settled mixed scene has 456 occupied grid cells; 90 dense cells produce 87% of 3.89 million raw pair checks. A warp per cell entry counts accepted pairs, then emits them in stable order without per-pair atomics. Same-state collision passes improved from 0.708 to 0.241 ms; both paths found the same 97,786 candidates. With the same three solver options, matched 180-frame headless OptiX runs improved from 49.95 to 51.59 rendered FPS (GPU starts below 10%). Two identical-setting 600-frame runs passed with zero fallen bricks and matched body, joint, and contact state byte for byte. An earlier diagnostic that toggled an unused legacy environment variable produced different final states, so repeatability beyond the identical-setting check remains under review. The grid mode is opt-in outside this scene.

Nsight Compute · biased contact iterate

Memory latency and low occupancy are the target

A captured regular-color launch spends 56.8% of issue intervals on long-scoreboard waits for L1/TEX data. Achieved occupancy is 7.9%, with 1.11 active and 0.08 eligible warps per scheduler; DRAM reaches only 16% and SM issue 2.4%. This is a single early-scene launch, so steady-state hardware counters still need confirmation. A byte-exact one-row lookahead reduced full-render FPS, so improving this dependency chain requires a different dataflow.

What does #1 do?

Each launch processes one conflict-free color shared by contacts and D6 joints. A revolute joint locks three translations and two rotations while leaving its hinge rotation free. Eight lanes evaluate each joint's matrix rows and one lane applies its equal and opposite impulses. Separate warps solve independent contact columns concurrently in the same launch. The trace has 96,000 launches, about 821 per rendered frame across substeps and sweeps. This kernel accounts for 60.5% of summed GPU kernel time.

Measured findings
Preparation overlap is neutral; small grid cells loseKeeping the fast concurrent solve kernel unchanged, assigning independent preparation warps to D6 joints and contacts measured 56.6 rendered FPS versus 56.7 FPS for solve-only overlap; that extra path was removed. An exact 0.3 m grid fell to 38.6 FPS from the 0.6 m grid's 56.7–58.2 FPS range, so the scene grid remains at 0.6 m.
Implicit tree rejected for this mixed sceneThe existing graph-capturable implicit-tree broad phase was substituted without changing scene geometry or solver settings. With the concurrent-family solve it reached only 14.7 rendered GL FPS over 148 measured frames, far below the grid path; the scene remains on the exact grid broad phase.
Concurrent joint and contact warps reach 58.2 rendered FPSRegular colors are conflict-free: their D6 joint range and contact range use separate warps inside the same kernel launch. This opt-in mapping measured 55.3–58.2 FPS across 177-frame rendered GL runs versus a fresh 47.1 FPS sequential-family run, with no extra launches. The 600-frame structural gate passed with 4.744 mm maximum attachment error, 1.46017° hinge-axis error, zero fallen bricks, at most 2.381 mm tower drop, and 32.0343 mm slide. Full body, joint, and contact state hashes matched the sequential path at frames 1, 30, 120, and 600; two independent 600-frame candidate runs also matched byte for byte. A GPU ownership regression catches duplicate joint work in joint-heavy colors. The 60 FPS goal is not yet met.
Leader-only D6 mass loads rejectedIn the eight-lane joint solve, loading inverse mass and inertia only for the impulse-scattering lane compiled and passed a short scene run. Matched 177-frame rendered GL throughput was 46.5 FPS versus 48.0 FPS for the current cooperative inverse path. The extra divergent body-load path outweighed the saved duplicate loads, so the prototype was removed.
Contact normal-block prototype rejectedA frozen-column oracle reduced local normal residual, but the GPU implementation slowed the unchanged ten-sweep rendered mixed scene from 47.4 to 39.5 FPS. It was removed before changing the production schedule; no full-scene convergence or stability gain was established.
Support-forest correction rejectedA maximum-impulse forest grounds all 11,340 tower bricks, but a stronger offline test gave it the later ten-sweep momentum change as an oracle target. Distributing that change across each selected multi-point patch increased weighted contact error 15.4× and body angular error 27.4×. Unilateral and friction limits bound 81% of patches; median feasible correction was only 0.23%. Paired momentum was conserved. This path has no production code or GPU speed claim.
Split-family register pressure offers littleCUDA reports 148 registers/thread for the mixed contact-and-joint iterate kernel, 134 for a contact-only specialization, and 146 for a joint-only specialization. Separate kernels would add launches and ordering work for only a modest contact register reduction; no production split was enabled.
Global Verlet pair caching misses the moving spheresA 240-physics-tick AABB-envelope probe found that a 2 cm skin still needs 206 grid rebuilds; a 10 cm skin needs 84 and would likely admit many extra narrow-phase candidates. The tower alone needs only two rebuilds at 2 cm. Whole-scene caching is a poor fit; a general per-island cache would need to handle cross-island candidates explicitly.
Independent humanoid pile confirms D6 throughput gainTwelve humanoids in one world, explicitly using maximal PGS D6 joints, improved from 52.7 to 67.1 rendered GL FPS over matched 117-frame runs with the cooperative inverse path. Its 600-frame bounded-state test passed. This benchmark changes that example's joint mode from its normal direct solve solely to exercise the supported colored path; the scene geometry and visual settings are the same.
Colibri confirms reuse across replicated worldsColibri's default four independent mechanisms use maximal PGS joints with temporal contact springs inside the single-world color-group dispatcher. The existing shared cooperative D6 path measured 95.8 rendered GL FPS versus 79.8 FPS with a temporary scalar override over 177 measured frames (+20.1%). The earlier 57-frame comparison also favored cooperative D6, 94.9 versus 79.1 FPS. No repository switch or scene setting changed. The separate multi_world dispatcher still rejects maximal_pgs joints.
Bike chain uses a different solverIts revolute joints are generic D6 rows solved through maximal_direct, so the colored PGS cooperative kernel does not execute there. The eight-lane joint math is already shared by the regular color-group and temporal-spring dispatchers, while their surrounding schedules stay separate because they handle different contact state and ordering.
Contact normal blocks expose a three-mode solveAt frame 120, 72,479 active contact columns contain 258,356 points; 86.8% are multi-point columns. A snapshot immediately after contact preparation validated reconstructed diagonal mobility for 4,448 of 4,487 sampled rows within 0.1%. In 1,024 sampled four- or five-point regular columns, 1,006 normal blocks have numerical rank three at a 1e-4 relative threshold. Scalar local normal GS leaves a median 12.9% residual after one sweep and 0.89% after eight on a reachable frozen demand. A proximal three-mode solve could remove local residual faster, but full friction, global FPS, and stability remain unmeasured; outliers and longer columns need fallback.
Generic eight-lane D6 solve gains 37% rendered FPSThe matched 177-frame GL run improved from 36.0 to 46.6 FPS by assigning eight GPU lanes to each D6 joint inside the existing color kernel. Computing pre-inverted D6 matrix rows across those lanes reached 49.3 FPS. No extra color launches or scene-specific coordinates are involved. The combined path passed 600 frames: zero fallen bricks, 2.38 mm maximum drop, 32 mm maximum slide, 4.744 mm joint attachment error, 1.460° hinge-axis error. The focused joint suite and deterministic replay passed. This remains opt-in while the new kernel profile is gathered.
Joint/contact component fusion is exact, but scene leverage is limitedA below-10%-GPU-start contact scan sampled frames 120, 240, 360, 480, and 600. Each sample found zero sphere-to-sphere columns and exactly four sphere-to-tower columns, all on one sphere; the other nine had only static contacts. At frame 120, a single-CTA-per-component kernel swept 4,209 rows across those nine spheres, including 429 ground-contact columns. Against the same rows launched once per color, it preserved body and copy velocities, joint impulses, contact impulses, and contact lambdas byte for byte, and cut isolated sweep time from 0.211 to 0.139 ms (34%). In a separate frozen-state 10-sweep solve screen, disabling these rows lowered 3.03–3.12 ms to 2.87 ms per substep. Across eight substeps per rendered frame, perfect overlap would save roughly 1.3–2.0 ms before scheduling cost, so this alone is unlikely to reach 60 FPS. No production component path is enabled.
Convergence screens retain the stable solveAt a below-10% GPU start, twelve regular colors and six sweeps lost four tower bricks by frame 600; the active nine-color, ten-sweep schedule is unchanged. A momentum-paired normal-only support-forest oracle made the frozen weighted normal-speed residual 15.4× worse after unilateral and friction clamping. Exact late-sweep skipping also lacks a safe trigger: by sweep ten, 81% of point impulses were unchanged but only 236 of 13,741 body velocities were unchanged, while over 80% of regular contact columns still carried nonzero impulses.
Iterate counters captured at lastThe corrected Nsight Compute run selected phoenx_singleworld_iterate. Long-scoreboard stalls account for 8.26 of 14.54 cycles between issued warp instructions (56.8%). Only 7.9% occupancy is achieved; 134 registers/thread cap theoretical occupancy at 25%, while the color launch provides just 0.67 full waves across SMs. The original 40% DRAM and 4% SM numbers belonged to contact preparation and are not used to diagnose iteration.
Mixed color-group kernel rejectedAn existing cooperative eight-lane D6 path keeps sequential colors inside independent groups. On this large mixed tower it managed only 3.0 headless GL FPS versus 35.1 FPS on the shared-color baseline. Its serial group work overwhelmed launch savings; the experiment was reverted.
Joint priority is not the convergence problemAt frame 120, all 4,200 constrained Hoberman joints occupy regular colors 0–6; zero are in the overflow partition. A joint-first color priority was tested and reverted after attachment error rose from 8.1 to 29.6 mm and hinge-axis error from 3.77° to 17°. The color order is preserved in the measured D6 inverse experiment below.
Precomputed D6 inverse improves rendered FPSAn opt-in FP64 inverse of each prepared joint block replaces repeated triangular solves during color sweeps. On the stable overflow-only scene, matched 180-frame headless GL runs measured 38.2 FPS with the inverse versus 36.1 FPS default over 177 rendered frames each. Both paths passed the 600-frame structural gate with zero fallen bricks. The inverse path measured 4.744 mm attachment and 1.460° hinge-axis error. Eleven focused joint, momentum, and determinism tests passed in each mode. Set PHOENX_EXPERIMENTAL_JOINT_INVERSE=1 to try the inverse.
Joints already lead each regular colorThe color sort groups D6 joints before contact columns. The persistent kernel gives lane 0 the first joint, then reuses the same lanes for the contact range. Separate joint-only and contact-only kernels gave clean family warps but added roughly 864 launches per rendered frame. A matched 120-frame GL test fell from 35.4 to 34.6 FPS, with matching joint errors and contact counts. The split was reverted.
FP64 friction is absent from the hot workloadA frame-30 census checked all 1,003,624 point rows against the solver's conditioning predicate. Only one zero-mobility point selected the fallback; none of the 649,213 loaded points did. Optimizing FP64 arithmetic cannot materially improve this Kapla case.
Warm starts mostly survive contact matchingAt frame 30, 88.8% of currently loaded points matched a previous contact and 88.2% matched a previously loaded normal impulse. The 12% unmatched tail leaves some convergence work, but broad warm-start failure is not the main cause of the 800 color launches per frame.

Broader options
Implicit tree wins on mixed sizesCaptured broad-phase replay with one tower and 1,000 boxes of 0.8–1.6 m: implicit BVTT 0.269 ms, SAP 0.590 ms, grid 0.729 ms. All three returned identical candidate-pair sets. Focused tree tests passed moving bounds, filters, touching faces, forced queue overflow, append behavior, and captured replay. Two independent 2×2 trajectories matched bitwise at frames 1, 24, and 48.
Grid wins on uniform KaplaWith four towers (45,360 bricks and 383,640 candidate pairs), grid replay took 0.215 ms, implicit BVTT 0.515 ms, and SAP 2.512 ms. Short stable-solver end-to-end OptiX replay reached 45.07 FPS with the tree versus about 49 FPS with the grid. The grid has no correctness volume cap: oversized and out-of-range shapes use its exact fallback.
Spatially resident solve has enough interior work to investigateAn offline frame-30 split of all 45,361 bodies by Morton order found 288,120 active contact columns. At 188 partitions (about one per GPU SM, 241 bodies each), 66.7% of all contacts and 66.0% of dynamic–dynamic contacts connect bodies inside the same partition. Each of the nine expensive regular colors has 67.6–72.2% interior contacts; overflow has 62.7%. At 94 partitions the all-contact interior fraction reaches 74.9%; at 376 it falls to 57.5%. An on-chip body-state solve is plausible for four towers, but a boundary-aware deterministic solver and full stability test are still required.
Balanced spatial partitions beat Morton chunksOn the frame-30 graph, a balanced recursive spatial split keeps 72.7% of contacts inside one of 188 partitions (241 bodies/partition), versus 66.7% for equal-size Morton chunks. Repartitioning frame 600 keeps 72.75%; retaining the initial body partition keeps 73.07%, so a first prototype needs no dynamic tree rebuild. With 94 partitions, the split keeps 79.5% internal at frame 30 and a fixed initial split keeps 79.34% at frame 600. Reverse Cuthill–McKee graph ordering reached only 55.2% at 188 partitions. These are offline topology measurements, not solver FPS or stability results.
Local recoloring fits a block sweep; GPU build is requiredA reusable frame-30 schedule prototype with fixed KD188 owners finds 210,728 of 288,120 contacts internal (73.14%), including 76,093 currently in the mass-splitting overflow bucket. Retaining nine global colors and recoloring internal overflow yields 13–28 local colors per partition (median 24). It classifies 77,391 boundary contacts and one inert pair. Its Python/NumPy schedule rebuild takes a median 102 ms from host arrays, so a GPU-only builder is mandatory. Neither solver FPS nor stability has been measured for this schedule.
GPU domain grouping passes; full recoloring is too costlyA captured GPU classify/sort/offset pipeline builds the fixed-KD188 domain order from live frame-30 contacts in 0.186 ms per replay. Adding deterministic local overflow recoloring and CSR construction raises this to 1.667 ms per build, before solving. Both outputs match an independent CPU build byte for byte. At eight substeps per rendered frame, rebuilding the full schedule could cost roughly 13.3 ms/frame, so the current experiment is looking for a domain solve that keeps the existing color and mass-split policy. Solver FPS and stability remain unmeasured.
Resident-domain sweep needs actual data reuseAn isolated frame-30 direct solve over 210,728 interior contacts became finite after those rows were prepared for direct solving. With original globally scattered contact data and no shared body cache, a captured 188-block local-color sweep took 0.264 ms; the production iterate profile averages about 0.198 ms per sweep across all contacts. The domain kernel omits 77,391 boundary contacts, so this version is slower. Domain fusion alone is insufficient; it must reduce indirect loads or improve occupancy. This is a one-sweep microbenchmark, not a stability or FPS result.
Block count helps only until boundary work dominatesIn a single sequential probe with a live frame-30 contact snapshot, 94 / 188 / 376 / 752 resident blocks solved 79.4% / 73.1% / 65.0% / 55.1% of columns in 0.376 / 0.302 / 0.216 / 0.288 ms per sweep. Boundary work was omitted, and the world state changed between trials, so these are screening numbers rather than matched FPS comparisons. More blocks raise available warps, but smaller domains lose too many interior contacts.
Preserving the existing color policy makes scheduling cheapA captured GPU builder groups only regular interior contacts by domain and original color, leaving boundary contacts in global colors and all 113,334 overflow contacts in the current mass-split path. It takes 0.218 ms per rebuild and matches an independent CPU ordering. This groups 134,635 regular interior contacts, with at most 889 columns or 242 bodies per domain. Velocity and angular velocity for a domain need at most 5.8 KB of shared memory. No end-to-end speed or stability claim follows yet.
Nsight's inactive lanes come mostly from the fixed launch sizeAt frame 30, 91.8% of the 174,786 regular contact columns have four or five contact points. Each regular-color kernel launches 48,128 threads, but the nine colors contain 22,112 down to 15,960 columns. The expected active lanes per warp from launch padding alone are 14.70 down to 10.61; the sampled color predicts 13.50 versus Nsight's 13.37. Sorting by point count cannot recover that gap.
Prior cooperative and shared-cache results close repeat experimentsThe repository's performance notes report that an eight-lane cooperative manifold kernel passed dynamics tests but reduced Kapla throughput from 83.7 to 78.2 FPS. A spatial shared-body-cache oracle improved its global-state version only about 1.1% at practical occupancy. Those results came from an earlier one-tower workload, so they are not direct four-tower FPS comparisons, but they make another broad cooperative or cache rewrite a poor next bet. The proposed four-lane extraction was stopped before changing solver code.
Shared manifold-frame compression fails the exact-equality gateA live frame-30 census checked normals and tangent directions within every multi-point contact column. Only 28,132 of 251,374 columns (11.2%) have an identical normal across all points; tangent equality is nearly the same. A common-frame flag and branch would save those reads too rarely to justify a production rewrite. The experimental census changed no solver state.
Contact rows are already dense and color orderedThe active solver gathers 1,003,624 frame-30 point rows into a dense prefix ordered by color and contact column; each manifold's points are contiguous. Its 34 float planes contain about 136.5 MB of active state, and canonical source indices preserve warm starts. A second full repack would add traffic. Prior ordinal-major and row-local layouts slowed matched or isolated runs, so another row permutation is closed as the immediate throughput path.
Coarse support correction has a measurable graph targetIn a live frame-30 four-tower census, vertically oriented contacts carry 98.0% of normal impulse weight. A maximum-impulse vertical support forest grounds 45,292 of 45,362 bodies (99.85%) with 45,293 of 288,120 contact columns; selected columns carry 50.5% of total normal impulse. Path depth is 10 at the median, 34 at p95, and 47 maximum. This suggests a long-range correction could improve ten-sweep convergence while retaining nine colors, but unilateral/friction correctness, build cost, residual reduction, FPS, and stability are unmeasured.
Fewer colors do not solve the stable workloadWith the required one velocity iteration and four substeps, one regular color reached 39.65 sustained OptiX FPS but collapsed all towers: 68–77% of bricks moved over 10 cm by frame 600. Four colors left 4.6–11.4% displaced and hundreds fallen per tower. Seven colors passed the 600-frame structural gate, but reached only 34.76 sustained OptiX FPS, essentially the nine-color reference. Color reduction is closed as a performance strategy; kernel latency and occupancy remain the target.
Exact color fusion lacks enough independent blocksAt frame 30 the dynamic contact graph has 49 connected components; the four largest each span about 7,100 bodies. Keeping today's update order without copies would leave at most 49 large thread blocks. Spatial groups would duplicate 26–65% of bodies at tested 2.0–0.5 m tile sizes, risking slower convergence. No fusion path was enabled.
The 62.16 FPS run is rejectedIt used two velocity iterations, violating the one-iteration rule, and the user observed visible instability. A separate run of that schedule also fell to about 9 FPS. Neither result establishes the current goal.
Quality gate
Two substeps are fast but fail lateEvery 20-frame window exceeded 60 FPS over 1,000 OptiX frames, yet only 50.7–72.0% of bricks per tower stayed within 1 cm over the final 100 physics frames. Friction-anchor drift and speed rise before contacts disappear.
End-of-run settling was an inadequate gateThe earlier metric counted bricks moving less than 1 cm during the final 100 frames. A fallen brick can pass once it comes to rest. The replacement gate will track displacement from the initial structure, tower height, escaped bricks, and motion across the run.
One velocity iteration is requiredThe two-tick, four-substep, ten-sweep run is the current structural reference. No bricks in its fourth tower met the 50 cm downward-fall test by frame 1,000, but a localized group shifted. Solver kernel work must preserve or improve this trajectory and paired-impulse momentum conservation.
